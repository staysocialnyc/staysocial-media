Why does J'ouvert start before sunrise? Blame an 1881 street fight in Port of Spain 🇹🇹

🔥 Canboulay began on the plantations — after emancipation it took over the streets
🔥 In 1881, Capt. Arthur Baker led about 150 police against Canboulay bands on Piccadilly St
🔥 The bands fought back with sticks, stones and bottles — 38 officers were hurt
🔥 The governor kept police in barracks for the rest of that Carnival
🔥 New rules for 1882 allowed torches only from midnight to 5 AM — the party moved to dawn
🔥 J'ouvert comes from 'jour ouvert': the day opens. The riot is re-enacted every Carnival Friday

Brooklyn's own J'ouvert joined Labor Day Carnival in the 1990s — moving before dawn down Flatbush Ave and across Empire Blvd.

Sources: UChicago Knowledge, National Trust of T&T, Trinidad Express, Library of Congress

Brooklyn has an official Little Caribbean — here's how it got the name 🇯🇲🇹🇹🇭🇹

📍 Designated in 2017, after a push led by Shelley Worrell — Brooklyn College grad, child of Trinidadian immigrants, founder of caribBEING
📍 Runs roughly from Empire Blvd south to The Junction, centered on Flatbush, Church and Nostrand avenues
📍 Won the 2024 Excellence in Historic Preservation Award from the Preservation League of New York State
📍 Its walking tours are supported by National Geographic and NYC Tourism + Conventions

Start at the I AM CARIBBEING HQ, 1399 Nostrand Ave, and book a walking tour at littlecaribbean.nyc.

Grenada's whole history fits in one pot 🇬🇩

🥥 Oil down: breadfruit, salted meat, vegetables and spices — the national dish, made for a crowd
🥥 The name comes from the coconut oil and meat juices that settle at the bottom of the pot
🥥 Breadfruit reached the Caribbean on an expedition led by Capt. William Bligh — the Mutiny on the Bounty captain
🥥 Callaloo and dasheen were grown by Grenada's earliest Amerindian people
🥥 Turmeric — called 'saffron' in Grenada — came with South Asian workers after emancipation in the 1830s
🥥 Locals say it matches the flag: green callaloo, red carrots, gold turmeric. And you layer it, never stir

Sources: NPR (via WAMC)

Spain, England, West Africa, India and China all helped make one Jamaican patty 🇯🇲

🥟 Spain brought the empanada, England the Cornish pasty — 'patty' and 'pasty' share a root
🥟 Enslaved African cooks reworked those recipes into something new
🥟 Turmeric gives the crust its gold; Indian indentured workers brought curry traditions after abolition
🥟 Chinese Jamaicans started two of the biggest chains, Tastee and Juici Patties, in the '60s and '70s
🥟 In 1985, Canadian inspectors said Toronto shops couldn't call it a 'patty' — the fix: add 'Jamaican'
🥟 Toronto still marks Feb 23 as Patty Day

NYC chapter: Golden Krust started here — Jamaican-born Lowell Hawthorne opened his first store in the Bronx in 1989.

Sources: Pier 21, CUNY Medgar Evers
